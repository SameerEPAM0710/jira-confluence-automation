# How to Complete the Status Report

1. Copy `template.md` and rename the copy for the project and reporting period. Keep the template unchanged for future reports.
2. Fill in the project name, reporting dates, preparer's name or role, and overall status.
3. Write the summary for a reader who needs the current health and outlook quickly. State what changed and why; avoid vague claims such as "making good progress."
4. List outcomes completed during the reporting period separately from planned work. Give each next-period item an owner and target date.
5. Update milestones with the latest target date and status. Use consistent status values such as `Not started`, `In progress`, `Complete`, or `Delayed`. Explain changes in the notes.
6. Record material risks and active issues with their impact, a concrete mitigation or next action, an accountable owner, and a due date. Remove resolved entries from the active list or explicitly mark them resolved.
7. List decisions or support needed, the person who can provide it, and the date by which it is needed. Write `None` if no decision or support is needed.
8. Order next-period priorities by importance and make them specific enough to verify.
9. Use absolute dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` format, keep wording concise, and check that the report period and milestone dates are consistent.
10. Set overall status using these definitions:
    - `On track`: planned outcomes and key dates remain achievable without material intervention.
    - `At risk`: an unresolved risk may affect scope, timing, or quality; recovery actions are underway or needed.
    - `Off track`: a key outcome or date is already missed or is unlikely to be met without a material change.
11. Before sharing, verify owners and dates, remove obsolete placeholders, and ensure the report contains no information its audience is not authorized to see.
